# Measuring the AI-deck camera: lab protocol

**What this is for.** `tools/crazysim_macos/camera_model.py` makes the simulator's
frames look like the real Himax HM01B0 sensor. Ten of its numbers are tagged
`UNMEASURED`: they come from the datasheet or from class experience, not from
*our* camera. Nobody has ever photographed anything through this pipeline. This
protocol turns as many of those ten as possible into measurements, in one lab
session, with printed paper and a tape measure.

**This is not the other protocol.** `docs/real_frame_capture_protocol.md` captures
frames of *people* to score the network. This one captures frames of *targets* to
measure the *sensor*. They share the drone, the tripod and `cpx_grab.py` and
nothing else. If time is short, the people protocol wins — it answers "does the
model work", this one answers "is the simulator honest".

> ## ⏱ How much of this actually happens in the first lab session: **10 minutes.**
>
> **Read this before you plan around the tier times below.**
> `docs/hardware/lab_session_runbook.md` is the session plan, and it gives this
> protocol **one 10-minute slot (S1, at 1:25)** inside a 2-hour session. The tier
> times in section 2 — 20 / 25 / 20 minutes — are honest estimates of this
> protocol run properly, and **they do not fit.** The runbook's slot is not going
> to grow, because the only place the time could come from is the people-frame
> capture, which is the reason the session is being held at all.
>
> **So the protocol is cut, deliberately, to its own ten-minute fallback:**
>
> | In the first session (S1, 10 min) | Deferred |
> |---|---|
> | **4.1** dark frame, **4.2** flat field — plus the runbook's two-minute bottle FOV | 4.3, and all of Tier 2 and Tier 3 |
> | Returns **6 of the 10** unmeasured parameters: `dsnu_sigma_dn`, `vignette_a2`, `vignette_a4`, `prnu_sigma`, `dead_px_frac`, `banding_depth`, plus a gain cross-check | `psf_sigma_center_px`, `psf_sigma_corner_px`, `e_per_dn_1x`, `ae_damping`, the accurate `focal_px_per_rad` |
> | **Needs no targets** — a lens cover and a sheet of printer paper | Needs the 600 mm board (Targets A and B) |
>
> **Consequence worth being blunt about: the white board is not on today's
> critical path.** If it does not get made, S1 still runs and still returns six
> parameters. Make it anyway — it is about $5 and it is the whole difference
> between 6 and 10 — but nobody should be improvising a board in the lab, and
> nobody should skip S1 because the board did not happen.
>
> **To get Tier 1 complete or Tier 1+2** the team has to choose one of: a longer
> slot, moving the flash-and-bench step to a second session, or a separate camera
> session (needs a drone and a room, no people and no consent). That is a team
> decision and neither document makes it.
>
> Run the tiers in order and stop when the slot ends — every tier, and inside
> Tier 1 every subsection, produces usable numbers on its own.

**Nothing here has been tested on hardware.** Every procedure below was
rehearsed against synthetic frames generated by `camera_model.py` itself, and
the arithmetic recovers the parameters that were injected (section 10). The
substitute is a good one for the arithmetic and no substitute at all for the
optics. Section 9 lists what could still go wrong on the day.

## 1. The ten parameters, and whether this lab can measure them

From the `UNMEASURED` tuple in `camera_model.py` (`grep -n '^UNMEASURED' -A 5`).
The tuple has **ten** names, not nine.

| # | Parameter | What it is | Measurable here? | How (section) | Sim value now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `psf_sigma_center_px` | Gaussian blur sigma applied at the frame centre: lens MTF convolved with the pixel aperture | **Yes** | slanted edge, 4.3 | 0.65 px |
| 2 | `psf_sigma_corner_px` | same, off-axis. **Recorded only** — the model applies one constant sigma everywhere | **Yes, but inert** | slanted edge aimed at a corner, 4.3 | 1.10 px |
| 3 | `vignette_a2` | r² term of the relative-illumination falloff, r = 1 at the corner | **Yes** | flat field, 4.2 | 0.22 |
| 4 | `vignette_a4` | r⁴ term of the same | **Yes, weakly** | flat field, 4.2 | 0.10 |
| 5 | `prnu_sigma` | photo-response non-uniformity, as a fraction of signal | **Yes** | level ladder, 5.1; flat field as cross-check | 0.008 |
| 6 | `dsnu_sigma_dn` | dark-signal non-uniformity in DN at 1x gain | **Yes, conditionally** | dark frame, 4.1 — *only if the dark frame is not clipped at 0* | 0.40 DN |
| 7 | `dead_px_frac` | defective-pixel clusters per pixel (the model injects one 2x2 cluster per `dead_px_frac`·H·W) | **Yes** | flat field, 4.2 | 1e-5 → one cluster |
| 8 | `ae_damping` | fraction of the exposure error the AE loop corrects per sensor frame | **Yes, roughly** | light step, 5.3 | 0.25 |
| 9 | `scene_light` | how bright the *simulated* scene is relative to the render | **No — not a sensor property.** What is measurable is the anchor it must reproduce: lux → total gain | lux ladder, 6.1 | 1.0 / 0.045 |
| 10 | `banding_depth` | depth of the row ripple from mains-driven light | **Yes** | flat field under room lights, 5.4 | 0.08 (low-light preset) |

Four more numbers the model treats as *exact* are worth checking while the drone
is on the bench, because everything else is built on them:

| Quantity | Where it appears | Measurable here? | How | Value assumed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `focal_px_per_rad` | bearing and blur length | **Yes, well** | two marks at two distances, 5.2 | 174.23 sim / 170.7 module |
| `e_per_dn_1x` | shot-noise law | **Yes** | photon transfer, 5.1 | 29 e⁻/DN |
| `read_noise_e` | read-noise law | **Only at high gain** | dark frame, 4.1 | 4.7 e⁻ |
| exposure time | motion blur | **Optional** | moving bar, 6.2 | AE-driven, ~4.3 ms in flight |

**Nothing here needs a register read.** That matters, because over the WiFi
streamer we cannot set or read a single sensor register. Every method below is
built around the one thing the sensor insists on doing: driving the frame mean to
60 DN. Where a method needs a different signal level, it gets it by changing what
is *in front of the camera*, never by changing the camera.

### 3.0 Set up the terminal — do this first, everything below assumes it

Every capture command in this document is written as a bare `cpx_grab.py ...`
writing into `"$S"`. Neither works in a fresh shell. Paste this block once, into
the terminal you are going to use all session, and then every command below can
be copy-pasted verbatim:

```sh
cd ~/Downloads/drone
export S=~/camera_measure/$(date +%F)
mkdir -p "$S"
cpx_grab.py()       { ~/Downloads/drone/trainenv/bin/python ~/Downloads/drone/pytorch_ssd/tools/crazysim_macos/cpx_grab.py "$@"; }
measure_camera.py() { ~/Downloads/drone/trainenv/bin/python ~/Downloads/drone/pytorch_ssd/tools/real_frames/measure_camera.py "$@"; }
echo "session folder: $S"
```

Check it took: `cpx_grab.py --help | head -3` should print usage, not
`command not found`. These definitions live **only in that terminal window** —
if it closes, or you open a second tab, paste the block again.

**Also check `echo "$S"` prints a path**, not an empty line. If `$S` is empty the
commands become `--out /dark`, `--out /flat` and so on. What happens then is worth
knowing exactly, because it is not what you would guess — verified by running it
on 2026-09-12 against a live stream:

```
connected to tcp://127.0.0.1:5151  labels: vis0_subj-dark_light-capped_take1
cannot create the output folder /dark: [Errno 30] Read-only file system: '/dark'
Pick a folder you can write to, e.g. --out ~/drone_frames/<date>
```

**`cpx_grab.py` connects to the deck first and only then fails on the folder**, so
you lose the clip, not just the command. It is still the loud way this goes wrong
rather than the quiet one — but the message says *read-only file system*, not
*permission denied*, and it arrives after a connection banner that looks like
success.

(The `cd ~/Downloads/drone` on the first line is not load-bearing — every path
inside the two functions is absolute, so they work from any directory. Verified
in **both zsh and bash** on 2026-09-12. Do **not** replace the functions with a
variable like `G="$P .../cpx_grab.py"`: that works in bash and fails in zsh,
which is this Mac's shell, with a baffling
`zsh: no such file or directory: /Users/.../python /Users/.../cpx_grab.py`.

`~/Downloads/drone/trainenv/bin/python .../cpx_grab.py` path every time and

   cpx_grab.py --n 5 --every 1 --out "$S"/check

   done as step **M1** at 0:15 — do not redo it, just re-read the log sheet.

   A rehearsal clip should report `324x244 depth=1 fmt=0` and `N saved` with N

   is not running** — start it before blaming the command.

   > `lab_session_runbook.md` section 2.2, which says to prefer `--port 5151`.**

### 4.1 Dark frame — dark offset, DSNU, read noise, hot pixels

**Do:** Cover the lens completely — tape over it, then a second layer. Any light
leak ruins it. Leave the room lights on (the cover is what matters).

```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 25 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject dark --light capped \
    --out "$S"/dark
```
That is 200–350 frames depending on how the WiFi link is behaving.

**Why it is worth doing:** in the dark the AE cannot reach 60 DN, so it winds
integration to the 340-line ceiling and then gain to the ceiling — `camera_model`'s
own loop lands on **24x total gain** there. That is the only operating point
where DSNU (0.4 DN at 1x) and read noise (0.162 DN at 1x) climb above the 8-bit
quantisation floor and become visible at all.

**The arithmetic** (all of it in `measure_dark`):

- *Temporal noise*: per-pixel variance from consecutive frame differences, with
  each difference's global and row means removed, halved. `var_t`.
- *Gain*: solve the model's own noise law for g —
  `var_t − 1/12 = S·g/29 + (0.162·g)²`, a quadratic in g, with S the mean dark
  level. Solving it rather than dropping the shot term matters: with a 22 DN
  pedestal the shot noise on the pedestal is a third of the variance, and
  ignoring it throws the gain out by 30%.
- *DSNU*: average all frames, high-pass with a 15x15 box to leave only per-pixel
  structure, sigma-clip the defects at 5σ, take the spatial variance, divide by
  the high-pass gain (1 − 1/15²) and subtract `var_t/N` for the temporal noise
  still in the average. `DSNU(1x) = sqrt(that) / g`.

**Expect:** mean well under 10 DN if the cover is good; temporal sigma 3–6 DN;
implied gain 15–25x; DSNU at 1x **0.1–1.0 DN**.

**The thing that can kill this measurement:** if the sensor has no black-level
pedestal, the dark frame is *all zeros* and there is nothing to measure — the
offset has been clipped away before it left the chip. The script detects this
(`frac_at_0 > 0.20`) and refuses to report a number rather than reporting a
confident zero. If that happens, DSNU comes from the level ladder (5.1) instead,
and you write "dark frame clipped, DSNU unmeasured from dark" on the sheet.
This is genuinely unknown until somebody looks.

### 4.2 Flat field — vignetting, PRNU, dead pixels

**Do:** Tape one sheet of plain white printer paper flat across the lens, 1–2 mm
from it. Point the drone at a bright, evenly lit white wall or at the ceiling
light. The paper is hopelessly out of focus at that range, which is the point:
it wipes out every scrap of scene texture and leaves only the lens's own falloff
and the sensor's own non-uniformity.

```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 20 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject flat --light diffuser \
    --out "$S"/flat
```
Then **roll the drone 180° about the lens axis** (upside down on the tripod),
keep it pointing at the same thing, and repeat:
```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 20 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject flat --light diffuser \
    --out "$S"/flat_roll180
```

The roll is a 30-second trick worth a lot: rotating the camera turns the scene
over on the sensor but leaves the lens falloff exactly where it was, so averaging
the two frames cancels any left–right or top–bottom gradient in the
illumination and leaves the real vignetting. If you skip it, the fit still runs
and the number is just less trustworthy; the report says which happened.

**The arithmetic:**

- *Vignetting*: on the roll-averaged frame, least squares
  `F ≈ c0 + c1·r² + c2·r⁴` with `r = 1` at the frame corner, then
  **`a2 = −c1/c0`, `a4 = −c2/c0`**. The free `c0` is there because the AE has
  already normalised the level, so only the shape carries information.
- *Dead pixels*: subtract a 5x5 local median from the averaged frame, flag
  anything past `max(8σ_robust, 10 DN)`, group with 8-connectivity.
  **`dead_px_frac = clusters / pixels searched`**, in `camera_model`'s own
  convention of clusters-per-pixel (it injects one 2x2 cluster per
  `dead_px_frac`·H·W, so 1e-5 today means literally *one* cluster).
- *PRNU*: same high-pass / sigma-clip / temporal-subtraction recipe as DSNU,
  then `prnu = sqrt(FPN_var − (DSNU·g)²) / level`.

**Expect:** level 55–65 DN (the AE target is 60); relative illumination
**0.75–0.90 at the side of the frame and 0.55–0.80 in the corner** (the model
says 0.82 and 0.68); `a2` 0.05–0.45; `prnu_sigma` 0.002–0.02; defect clusters
**0–20**.

**Wild results to catch on the spot:** a relative corner illumination above 0.95
means the "flat" was not flat — most likely the paper is not covering the whole
field. Hundreds of defect clusters means the frame is not settled: check the AE
had a couple of seconds before the clip.

### 4.3 Slanted edge — PSF sigma at the centre and the corners

**Do:** Stand Target A (Appendix A: the big white board with matte black card
over one half, boundary tilted about 5°) flat against the wall. Put the drone
**0.45 m** from it, square on — close enough that the board covers most of the
frame, so a corner-aimed edge still has board behind it. Aim so the boundary
runs through the middle of the frame.

```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 6 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject edge --light room \
    --out "$S"/edge
```
Then pan and tilt the tripod so the edge runs through the **top-left corner** of
the frame, and again for the **bottom-right**:
```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 6 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject edge --light room \
    --out "$S"/edge_corner_a
cpx_grab.py --seconds 6 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject edge --light room \
    --out "$S"/edge_corner_b
```

**You do not have to type any coordinates.** The script sweeps a 5x4 lattice of
72-pixel windows over every `edge*` folder and keeps each window that contains
one clean, properly tilted edge. Aim roughly; it finds them. On the rehearsal
data it finds 16 usable edges from two clips.

**The arithmetic** (`lsf_variance`), which is ISO-12233-style slanted-edge
super-resolution done by hand:

1. Fit the edge to a straight line, sub-pixel, using the **signed** derivative
   inside a ±5 px window. (Using `|derivative|` over the whole row — the obvious
   thing — is badly biased: rectified noise gives every flat pixel a small
   positive weight and drags the centroid inward. On rehearsal frames that alone
   pulled the fitted tilt from 0.0875 to 0.054 px/row and *tripled* the measured
   blur.)
2. Project every pixel onto the edge normal, bin at 0.1 px → a super-resolved
   edge spread function.
3. Difference it → the line spread function; take its second moment in a window
   re-sized from the width it just measured; subtract the two 0.1²/12 terms the
   binning put in.
4. Convert that variance into the number to type into the preset by inverting
   `camera_model`'s *sampled* Gaussian kernel, not by taking a square root.
   Below sigma ≈ 0.7 the sampled kernel's variance is far under sigma² (at 0.3
   it is 0.008, not 0.09), so a square root would be plainly wrong.

A second, independent estimate from an erf fit to the ESF is printed alongside.
The two agreeing is the sign the edge was clean.

**Expect:** LSF sigma **0.4–1.6 px** at the centre, the same or larger in the
corners; edges found out to r ≈ 0.8 of the corner radius. Contrast 80–150 DN.
Edge tilt **2–25°** — a perfectly vertical edge cannot be super-resolved at all
and the script refuses it.

**Wild results:** sigma under 0.3 px means the boundary was not resolved as an
edge (window straddling two edges, or the board too far); sigma over 2.5 px
means the camera is **out of focus at 0.45 m**. The AI-deck lens is fixed-focus
and nobody here knows where it is set. If you see that, move the old `edge/`
aside, re-shoot the centre clip at 0.9 m, and compare — the sharper distance is
the one to believe, and which one it is, is itself a finding worth writing down.

### 5.1 Level ladder — conversion gain, read noise, PRNU and DSNU together

This is the most informative twenty minutes in the protocol, and the trick is
worth understanding before you do it.

**The problem:** the sensor's auto-exposure always parks the frame *mean* at
60 DN. There is no way to ask for another signal level over the WiFi link — any
uniform change in light is exactly undone by the AE.

**The trick:** the AE meters the *whole frame*. So put a flat white patch on a
black surround and change how much of the frame the patch fills. The AE keeps the
mean at 60 by raising the exposure, which carries the patch up to 100, 170,
220 DN. The camera is now a four-point transfer curve and nothing was
reconfigured.

**Do:** Stand Target B (Appendix A: one piece of matte white board, about
600 x 600 mm) against a **matte black backdrop** — black poster board, a
blackout curtain, a dark open doorway. Drone square on.

Take four clips, moving the tripod back between each, so that the **white board's
width** fills roughly the whole frame, then 70%, then 57%, then 49% of it. Judge
it from a saved frame, not by eye through nothing. For a 600 mm board that is
about 0.32, 0.46, 0.57 and 0.66 m, and it puts the patch at roughly 60, 115,
165 and 210 DN.

```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 10 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject level --light room \
    --out "$S"/level_01     # board fills the frame
cpx_grab.py --seconds 10 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject level --light room \
    --out "$S"/level_02     # board ~70% of frame width
cpx_grab.py --seconds 10 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject level --light room \
    --out "$S"/level_03     # ~57%
cpx_grab.py --seconds 10 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject level --light room \
    --out "$S"/level_04     # ~49%
```
Write the four distances on the sheet. **Check after each clip that the patch is
not blown out** — if it reads 255 the level carries no information at all and
the script drops it; three unsaturated levels is the minimum.

**The arithmetic** (`measure_ladder`), over the central part of each patch:

- *Photon transfer:* `var_temporal(S) = S·g/29 + (0.162g)² + 1/12`, so a straight
  line of temporal variance against signal has
  **slope = g/29 → e⁻/DN at the operating gain = 1/slope**, and
  **intercept − 1/12 = read noise²**.
- *Fixed-pattern transfer:* `var_fpn(S) = prnu²·S² + (DSNU·g)²`, so a straight
  line of spatial fixed-pattern variance against **S²** has
  **slope = prnu²** and **intercept = (DSNU·g)²**.

The second of these is why the ladder is the primary PRNU source: it separates
the multiplicative pattern from the additive one *at the same gain*, needing
neither a dark frame nor the assumption that DSNU scales with gain.

**Expect:** four levels spread over 60–230 DN; both fits with R² above 0.95;
**e⁻/DN between 15 and 45** (the model says 29); `prnu_sigma` 0.002–0.02.

**The honest limit, and the script says so:** at 1x gain the read noise is
0.162 DN = 0.026 DN², which is *below* the 1/12 DN² that 8-bit rounding
contributes. It cannot be measured in a bright room. When the fitted intercept
sits inside its own error bar, the script assumes g = 1 and prints an upper
bound instead of inventing a gain. That assumption is sound exactly when the room
was bright enough that the AE never reached for gain — and a small intercept is
itself the evidence that it did not.

### 5.2 Field of view — the focal length, done properly

`focal_px_per_rad` scales every bearing the follower computes, and the sim's
174.23 and the module's 170.7 differ by 2%. To beat that, the measurement has to
beat 2%, and the thing standing in the way is that **you cannot measure the
distance to the entrance pupil** — the tape reads to the tripod, and the optical
reference point is somewhere inside the lens, tens of millimetres away.

**The fix is to measure at two distances and subtract.** With `s` the image scale
in pixels per metre of target and `δ` the unknown pupil offset,
`1/s = (D + δ)/f`, so

> **f = (D₂ − D₁) / (1/s₂ − 1/s₁)**, and δ falls out as a by-product.

Only the *difference* of the two distances enters, and that you can measure
exactly by sliding the tripod along a tape.

**Do:** Put two strips of black tape on a white wall, vertical, about 30 mm wide,
with their **centres exactly 1.000 m apart** (measure centre to centre twice).
Drone level and square on, marks at the same height as the lens.

```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 4 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject fov --light room \
    --out "$S"/fov          # tripod at 0.70 m
cpx_grab.py --seconds 4 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject fov --light room \
    --out "$S"/fov_far      # tripod at 1.40 m
```
At 0.70 m the two marks land about 250 px apart and at 1.40 m about 125 px — both
comfortably inside a 324 px frame, with the long lever arm that makes the
subtraction worth doing.
Write both distances into `session.json`. Get them from the same fixed point on
the tripod both times — the difference is what matters, not the absolute value.

**Expect:** `focal_px_per_rad` **160–185**, full HFOV **82–90°**. The implied
pupil offset should come out within about ±30 mm; more than that is a measuring
mistake, not optics. The single-distance number printed above it will differ —
that difference *is* the pupil offset, and it is the reason for the second clip.

**Optional distortion check:** if you also print Target C (bar sheet) and shoot
it filling the frame, the script fits the bar positions with a free tilt and a
free `k1`, and reports the residual. A fit RMS above about 0.5 px is real
distortion. `camera_model` has a `k1` knob currently set to 0. Note the sign
convention: the reported `k1_forward` maps ideal → observed, while
`camera_model`'s `k1` is the sampling-side coefficient, roughly its negative.

### 5.3 AE step response — `ae_damping`

**Do:** Point at the flat wall, room lights on, and start a long clip. While it
runs, make the **biggest sudden change in light you can that does not drive the
frame to black or to 255** — a desk lamp aimed at the wall switched off and on,
or a sheet of white paper swung in front of the lens and away. Hold each state
for about two seconds, and do it five times.

```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 25 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject aestep --light room \
    --out "$S"/ae_step
```

A factor of five to ten is what this wants. You can tell you got it: the script
reports the points it used per step, and a step that only yields two is too
small to fit. If it says so, make the next one bigger.

**The arithmetic:** the loop multiplies exposure by `(target/measured)^frac` once
per camera frame, with `frac = 1 − (1−d)^n` over the `n` sensor frames that
elapsed. In log terms the residual `u = ln(mean/60)` just decays geometrically,
`u_{k+1} = (1−d)^n·u_k`. So fit `ln|u|` against time, then

> **d = 1 − (exp(slope·Δt))^(1/n)**,  `n = round(Δt / 16.59 ms)`

using the clip's own median frame interval Δt. Dividing by `n` rather than
assuming one sensor frame per captured frame is what removes the rounding bias
the model's own `round(dt / 16.59 ms)` introduces.

Only points more than 6% away from the target are used: the loop latches
"converged" once the error is inside 3 DN and stops correcting, and a latched
point flattens the tail and drags the fitted damping down.

**Expect:** 3–5 usable steps, at least 3 points each, **`ae_damping` 0.1–0.5**,
and the steps agreeing with each other within about ±0.05. Over the WiFi link
you get only ~13 frames a second while the loop runs at 60, so each step gives
three or four points and no more: this is **the least precise number in the
protocol**. Call it ±30% and write that next to it.

**Wild results:** "0 usable steps" means the steps were too small, or the clip
saturated — the log law only holds between about 4 and 250 DN, and the script
throws away everything outside that.

### 5.4 Banding — `banding_depth`

Flicker avoidance is off on this sensor (`FS_CTRL = 0x00`), so mains-driven light
plus the rolling shutter should put a row-wise ripple on every frame with a
random phase, at a predicted period of 268 rows — of which only 0.91 of a cycle
fits in 244 rows.

**Do:** nothing extra if the `flat/` clip was taken under the room's own lights;
the script falls back to it. Better, take a dedicated clip with the diffuser on
the lens pointed straight at a ceiling fixture:
```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 20 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject banding --light room \
    --out "$S"/banding
```
If the room has a dimmer, take a second clip dimmed — banding is usually far
stronger on a dimmed fixture.

**The arithmetic:** row-mean profile per frame, divided by the profile averaged
over all frames (random phase averages the ripple out of the reference), then
least squares for `A·cos(2πy/268 + φ)` per frame with a free offset and ramp.
**`banding_depth` = median A.** The same fit at a period nothing is happening at
(37 rows) is reported as a control, and a sweep over periods — scored by
*variance explained*, not amplitude, because amplitude runs away at long
periods — says whether the ripple really sits near 268 rows.

**Expect:** either a clear ripple, depth **0.02–0.20** with the period sweep
landing in 200–350 rows, or nothing at all. **Nothing at all is a real result:**
a modern DC-driven LED fixture does not flicker, and for that room
`banding_depth = 0` is correct. The script says which, by comparing against the
control.

## 6. Tier 3 — only if everything else is done (20 minutes)

> **Definitely not in the first session.** Recorded here so the method exists for
> whenever a dedicated camera session happens.

### 6.1 Lux ladder — the `scene_light` anchor

`scene_light` cannot be measured, because it is not a property of the camera: it
says how bright the *simulated* scene is relative to the render. What *can* be
measured is the thing it has to reproduce — **how much gain the real AE reaches
for in a room of a given brightness**.

**Do:** diffuser on the lens, pointed at the wall. Take a 15-second clip at each
light level you can make (all lights, half, one lamp, dark corner). At each,
hold the phone's light meter at the wall and write down the lux. Name the folders
by the reading:
```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 15 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject lux --light room \
    --out "$S"/lux_420        # 420 lux
```

**The arithmetic:** the gain follows from the noise (same quadratic as 4.1), and
the simulator's own AE then gives, for a render whose mean is R DN,

> **`scene_light` = 60 / (R · g)**

With the default R = 165 DN that is 0.364/g — which puts the existing
`himax_low_light` value of 0.045 at g = 8.1, exactly the "~8x total gain" the
preset is specified by. The formula is consistent with what is already there,
which is the reason to trust it.

**Expect:** gain 1x in a normally lit room, rising into the 4–20x range as you
dim. **Phone lux meters are uncalibrated to roughly ±30%** — the *ratios* between
levels are far more trustworthy than the absolute numbers, and the gain column is
measured independently of the phone anyway.

### 6.2 Exposure time from motion blur

The model computes blur length from the gyro and the exposure time; the exposure
time is the part nobody has ever checked.

**Do:** open a full-screen page on the laptop showing a white bar sliding
horizontally across black at a known speed (Appendix A, Target D — copy the
snippet into a `.html` file). Put the drone at a measured distance, aim at the
screen, and capture 5 seconds. Work out the bar's **image speed in px/s** —
`(bar speed in m/s / distance in m) × focal_px_per_rad` from 5.2 — and put it in
`session.json` as `blur.px_per_s`.

```
cpx_grab.py --seconds 5 --every 1 --vis 0 --subject blur --light room \
    --out "$S"/blur
```

**The arithmetic:** the moving edge is the static edge smeared by a box of length
L, so `var_moving − var_static = L²/12`, and **`t_exp = L / v_image`**. The static
variance comes from the `edge/` clip of 4.3.

**Expect:** **1–10.6 ms**, i.e. 32–340 integration lines — 340 lines is the
sensor's `MAX_INTG` ceiling, so anything above 10.6 ms means the arithmetic or
the bar speed is wrong, not the camera. The model's control flight ran at
137 lines = 4.26 ms.

## 9. What cannot be measured this way

Being clear about these is the point of the document. None of them are reachable
with paper targets and a tape measure.

| Cannot measure | Why | What it would take |
|---|---|---|
| Absolute full well (7400 e⁻) | The AE never lets the photodiode near saturation; we only ever see the 8-bit output clip | Register access to pin exposure and gain, then drive the sensor into saturation |
| Analog vs digital gain split | Not observable in the pixel stream; the model uses total gain for both noise terms, so it is physically inert there anyway | Reading `AGAIN`/`DGAIN` over I²C from a modified GAP8 app |
| `e_per_dn_1x` and `read_noise_e` *separately at 1x* | At 1x the read noise is below the quantisation floor. Section 5.1 gets their ratio; separating them needs the high-gain point | A firmware build that fixes gain at a known step, or a calibrated light source |
| Exposure time directly | No register read; 6.2 infers it from a moving target and inherits that target's speed error | Register access, or a strobe of known frequency |
| The sensor's true 60.3 fps frame rate | The WiFi stream runs at ~13 fps and is not the sensor's own rate | Register access, or an oscilloscope on the frame-valid line |
| Quantum efficiency, dark current vs temperature | Needs a calibrated light source and a temperature-controlled chamber | An actual optics lab |
| `scene_light` itself | It is a simulator knob, not a camera property (section 6.1 measures its anchor instead) | Nothing — it is a modelling choice, and should stay tagged `UNMEASURED` |
| `psf_sigma_corner_px` *having any effect* | The model applies one constant sigma to the whole frame; the corner value is recorded and never used | A code change in `camera_model.py` to make the PSF radial — a separate decision, not this protocol's |

Two more honest caveats about numbers this protocol *does* produce:

- **DSNU referred to 1x** assumes DSNU scales with gain. That is what
  `camera_model` assumes and it is right for dark-current-driven DSNU, but an
  offset introduced *after* the gain stage would not scale, and we cannot tell
  the two apart from outside.
- **A colour (Bayer) deck** changes several of these. The script detects the
  mosaic from the flat field and computes per-pixel statistics per CFA phase,
  but the PSF and FOV numbers then describe the mosaicked frame, not the
  firmware's 2x2-averaged luma. Write "colour deck" in big letters on the sheet.

photon-transfer slope is the measurement that tests it — if the fitted e⁻/DN
comes back far from 29, believe the sensor, not the model, and say so loudly.

## Appendix A — what to make, and how big

The AI-deck's field of view is wide: about **86° across**, so at distance *D* the
frame is **1.86·D** wide and **1.40·D** tall. That is why the useful targets are
**board-sized, not sheet-sized** — an A4 sheet only fills the frame at 16 cm,
which is closer than a fixed-focus lens is likely to be happy with.

| At this distance | the frame is | an A4 sheet covers | a 600 mm board covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.32 m | 600 x 450 mm | 49% of the width | the whole frame |
| 0.45 m | 840 x 630 mm | 35% | 71% |
| 0.70 m | 1300 x 980 mm | 23% | 46% |
| 1.00 m | 1860 x 1400 mm | 16% | 32% |

Printed sheets still print at **100% scale / "actual size"**, never "fit to
page", and get checked with a ruler before you leave for the lab.

**The white board — Targets A and B both use it** *(for 4.3 and 5.1)*
**One single piece** of matte white card or board, about **600 x 600 mm**: white
poster board, foam board, flip-chart paper, or the blank back of a poster.

*One piece is not a style preference.* A seam between two taped-together sheets
is a thin dark line, and a thin dark line is per-pixel structure that the
fixed-pattern measurement will faithfully record as sensor non-uniformity. For
the same reason the board must be **unprinted** — printer halftone texture is
exactly what the PRNU measurement is trying to see. Matte, not glossy: a gloss
surface throws a specular highlight of the room's lights straight into the
frame.

- **As Target B** (5.1, the level ladder) it is used bare, against a **matte
  black backdrop** at least 1 m across — black poster board, a blackout curtain,
  or a dark open doorway.
- **As Target A** (4.3, the slanted edge) tape **matte black card over one half
  of it**, deliberately crooked by about **5°** — over a 600 mm height that is a
  52 mm offset between the top and bottom of the boundary, so it is easy to eye
  in. The tilt is the whole trick: a perfectly vertical edge cannot be
  super-resolved, and the script refuses one. Anything from 2° to 25° works.

**Two tape marks** *(for 5.2, nothing to print)*
Two strips of black electrical tape on a white wall, vertical, about 30 mm wide,
their **centres exactly 1.000 m apart**. Measure centre to centre twice, tape
held flat against the wall. This is the single most accuracy-critical number in
the whole protocol and it takes thirty seconds — the focal length is directly
proportional to it.

**Target C — bar sheet** *(optional, the distortion check in 5.2)*
One A4 sheet, **landscape**: vertical black bars **20 mm wide with 20 mm white
gaps**, so the edge-to-edge pitch is **20 mm**. That fits **6 bars, 12 edges**
across the ~277 mm a normal printer will actually put ink on — which is just
above the 6 edges the fit needs, so the distortion check is coarse by
construction. Shoot it at **0.35 m**, where each bar is about 10 px wide.
*Ruler check:* six full periods (black + white) should measure **240 mm ± 1 mm**.
Printers scale; put the **measured** pitch into `session.json` as
`fov.edge_pitch_m`, not the nominal one.

**Target D — moving bar** *(optional, 6.2)*
Not printed — copy this into `~/blurbar.html` and open it full-screen on the
laptop. It sweeps a white bar left to right once per second; measure your
screen's width in millimetres with the tape, and the bar's speed is
`screen_width_mm / 1000 ms`.

```html
<style>
  html,body{margin:0;height:100%;background:#000;overflow:hidden}
  #b{position:absolute;top:0;height:100%;width:6vw;background:#fff;
     animation:s 1s linear infinite}
  @keyframes s{from{left:-6vw}to{left:100vw}}
</style>
<div id="b"></div>
```
